06 / Business insights

Mixed-brand baskets are larger

Both-brand baskets represent 57.0% of baskets and 80.28% of sales. Their larger basket size is an association, not proof of cause.

Interpretation

Mixed-brand baskets average 14.58 lines, compared with 3.09 in National-only baskets and 1.27 in Private-only baskets. Larger shopping missions explain the observed concentration; brand mixing itself has not been shown to increase spend.

07 / Business recommendations

Next steps grounded in evidence

These are opportunities to investigate, not claimed financial improvements or proven causal effects.

01

Investigate Private-label opportunities in Grocery

Evidence: Private Grocery recorded $1,105,832.67 (27.01% of Grocery sales); 2,490 households bought Private Grocery products.

Compare like-for-like products, price, availability and gross margin before considering a controlled merchandising or promotion test. Track revenue share and margin against a suitable comparison.

02

Explore basket-building opportunities

Evidence: mixed-brand baskets are 57.0% of baskets and 80.28% of sales, with 14.58 lines per basket.

Investigate co-purchased categories and test relevant placement or combinations only where stock and margin data support it. Compare against a control; do not treat current basket differences as incremental lift.

03

Improve promotion data quality before uplift analysis

Evidence: 78.3% of lines have no matching promotion record, and 15,245 product-store-week keys have conflicting states.

Reconcile mappings, resolve conflicts and verify code definitions before defining exposure. Better coverage is necessary, but it does not by itself establish causality.

Important limitations

The analysis does not provide verified product margins, causal impact, promotion uplift or return on investment. Days and weeks are relative; store IDs have no verified locations; manufacturer IDs are not verified supplier identities.

Technical context and data-grain notes

Pipeline: source CSVs → PostgreSQL → dbt staging and analytical models → Power BI Import model. Sales lines, baskets and coupon redemptions remain at separate grains. Facts are not joined to each other, preventing duplicated revenue and conflated redemption events.
